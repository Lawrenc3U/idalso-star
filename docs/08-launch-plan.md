# Stage 8 — Launch plan

## Store presentation

Working title: **Yardwork: Prison Mining**. Check availability, search results and trademark conflicts before adoption. Short description: “Welcome to the world's most cheerful workshop yard! Mine colourful ore, fill your backpack, sell your haul and earn new ranks. Upgrade your pickaxe, discover brighter mines and work toward prestige. All progression is available through play.” Only include the final sentence about prestige when expanded mode is actually released.

Icon brief: original orange-uniform cartoon avatar, oversized teal pickaxe and one mint-glowing ore block against a warm courtyard. Strong silhouette, readable at small sizes, no copied character designs, fake rewards or Robux imagery. Prototype icon must depict the actual part-based game, not a quality level or feature it cannot deliver.

Thumbnail briefs: (1) Mine → Sell → Upgrade in three readable panels; (2) before/after courtyard mine with actual ranks shown; (3) cooperative mining with original guard reaction. After expanded release add a prestige thumbnail. Capture real in-game footage or clearly faithful staged scenes. Test one variable at a time once traffic supports useful comparison.

## Pricing hypotheses (Robux; not published prices)

| Offering | Initial proposal | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2× Money | 149 | Permanent disclosed acceleration |
| 2× Tokens | 149 | Only eligible earned prestige tokens |
| Auto-target | 99 | Free tap/hold remains; no offline income |
| +50% backpack | 99 | Earned backpack upgrades still available |
| VIP | 79 | Tag and cosmetic lounge |
| Starter pack | 49 | 500 money + gold cosmetic; repeat product semantics must be clear |
| 2,000 / 12,000 money | 29 / 99 | Fixed amounts shown, no surprise scaling |
| 15 / 30 / 60 minute 2× boost | 29 / 49 / 79 | Time extends; strongest minutes-per-Robux is 60-minute option |
| Reset credit | 19 | Only after recovery and shared warning tests pass |

Prices are hypotheses, not API-set prices or revenue forecasts. Verify current fees, eligibility, regional pricing, product/pass rules, ad requirements and monetization programmes on Creator Hub. No historical payout programme is assumed. Review net proceeds and refunds in actual platform reporting before any acquisition-spend decision.

Store presentation is opt-in. No forced purchase prompts, countdown pressure, fake discounts, intentionally slow free controls or paid-only progression walls. Do not sell an Auto pass as if free touch hold requires payment. Show why a 60-minute boost is labelled best-value only using a valid actual price comparison; the current UI intentionally does not assert an unverified discount.

## Three-month roadmap after private validation

| Period | Focus | Exit evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | Private A–C playtests, onboarding, touch controls, mining feel | Repeatable complete loop and safe saves; fix friction first |
| Weeks 3–4 | Small public free prototype if gates pass; collect baselines | Stable sessions, measured progression, support process |
| Weeks 5–6 | Expand ranks/mine art gradually; test full prestige privately | Economy reconciles expected timing and real player data |
| Weeks 7–8 | Expanded progression plus token upgrades, rewards, basic cosmetics | Multi-session saves, prestige and exploit gates pass |
| Weeks 9–10 | Enable verified passes/products gradually | Real fulfilment/replay tests pass; monitor failures |
| Weeks 11–12 | One scheduled Workshop Weekend; improve icons and social feedback | Compare retention/loop metrics, rollback if harmful |

Dates are a solo-developer planning envelope; cut scope or delay when a gate fails. Pets beyond the basic cosmetic buddy, complex collections, new environments and further live ops belong after this foundation. Do not promise weekly content before production capacity is known.

## Measurement and operations

Track first-sale completion, time to first upgrade/rank, bag friction, D1/D7/D30, session length, purchase conversion and source/sink totals. Inspect the largest onboarding drop first. Set alert habits for datastore errors, receipt deferrals, lock contention, reset stalls and exploit reports. Do not promise profit or infer causal retention improvements from a tiny sample.

Keep a private test place, prior published versions, datastore namespace strategy and a written release checklist. Roll back application updates when errors rise; do not blindly restore old player records or remove pending receipt IDs. Announce downtime plainly, compensate with bounded claims when appropriate, and use only age-appropriate platform-supported community channels. External community links must comply with current age and link policies.
